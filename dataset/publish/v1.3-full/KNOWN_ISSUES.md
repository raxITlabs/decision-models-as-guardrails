# Known issues in v1.3

Found after the benchmark ran, mostly by the data-quality review of 24 September 2026. The released rows, labels and splits are unchanged; each issue says what it changes and where the correction lives.

- **Metadata correction (label_policy).** Was: provisional: denied topics and B2 pairs carry single-AI reference labels (label_basis llm, review_status ai_reviewed). Profanity uses Civil Comments' original obscene rater fractions as a derived binary label (>= 0.5 yes, 0 no, intermediate excluded). PII comes from NVIDIA Nemotron-PII source spans with every negative blind-reviewed. Not publication approval Now: Denied topics and B2 pairs carry labels drafted by a single AI reviewer (label_basis llm, review_status ai_reviewed, kept as their origin); the project owner reviewed all current labels on 28 September 2026 (owner-review-confirmation.json), which is owner review, not independent two-reviewer adjudication. Profanity uses Civil Comments' original obscene rater fractions as a derived binary label (>= 0.5 yes, 0 no, intermediate excluded). PII comes from NVIDIA Nemotron-PII source spans with every negative blind-reviewed. Not publication approval
- **pii-source.** PII rows come from NVIDIA Nemotron-PII (CC-BY-4.0, test split), replacing AI4Privacy. Labels are the source's generated spans, mapped to nine entity types and audited on a 100-document sample (99 confirmed). Every negative passed a screen for gaps the audit found (fax numbers, SSNs on international rows, IPs inside URLs) and a blind AI review. Driver's licence numbers are not measured: the source has no such label. IP address, password and SSN appear in few test rows, so no per-type claim is made. The negatives' blind review was AI; the project owner later reviewed all current labels.
- **gandalf-subtype.** All Gandalf rows are mapped to subtask leakage. The source selects instruction-override attempts by embedding similarity and has no per-row leakage label. Treat as a noisy attack proxy; no leakage-specific claim.
- **content-taxonomy.** 32 of 240 selected content test positives map in our taxonomy to PII (21) or TOPIC (11) rather than the five content categories. Content measures agreement with the sources' policies, not only the five content filters.
- **label-review.** Denied topics and B2 pairs were labelled by a single AI reviewer; each row keeps that origin (label_basis llm, review_status ai_reviewed). On 28 September 2026 the project owner reviewed all current labels in this release and changed none (owner-review-confirmation.json, LABEL_REVIEW.json). Owner review, not independent two-reviewer adjudication; no inter-rater agreement is measured.
- **profanity-derived.** Profanity rows (civil_comments_obscene) use Civil Comments' crowd-rater obscene share: yes at 0.5 or more, no at 0. 14,800 comments in between and 552 the benchmark had already used were excluded. Derived binary labels, not unanimous judgments; the hardest borderline cases are absent. For Bedrock's managed profanity filter the score is performance against an external dataset, not identical implementation or vocabulary.
- **profanity-lexicon-set.** The 36 lexicon-selected masked-spelling rows (civil_comments_profanity, subtask profanity_obfuscated, AI labels) remain as a diagnostic. The rest of that lexicon-selected set is in release v1.2. Neither is part of the profanity score.
- **b2-size.** B2 has 8 kept pairs: 4 test pairs in 3 groups. Individual cases, not a rate.
- **bias-three-parts.** Added 29 September 2026. Earlier material grouped every bias test under one 'Bias' heading. They answer three questions. Hate and discrimination detection is the content rows whose source labels them hateful or discriminatory, scored once as content. Guardrail fairness diagnostics are B1 (b1_disparate_fpr) and B2 (b2_counterfactual), which ask whether false blocks, missed violations or decisions on swapped texts differ across identity groups. Decision-model bias diagnostics are BBQ and discrim-eval (b3_decision), which ask whether a decision model makes unsupported stereotyped assumptions. B1 labels are Civil Comments toxicity, not discrimination labels; an identity mention sets a row's group, never its label. Our taxonomy files some Aegis 2.0 Harassment and Profanity rows and OpenAI moderation HR rows under category.bedrock HATE. No row, label, split or config changes. A content score is not a fairness percentage, and catching hate does not show a system is free of bias. To study hate detection alone, select rows by category.source_label. A guardrail that only blocks or allows cannot answer the b3_decision tasks.
