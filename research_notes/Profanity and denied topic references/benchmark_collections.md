# Guardrail benchmark collections traced to original datasets: profanity and specialised-advice / denied-topic labels

Research date: 2026-09-28. Revision hashes were read live from the Hugging Face Hub API (`/api/datasets/<id>` → `sha`) and from `git ls-remote <repo> HEAD` on that date. Label values marked "(observed)" were read from the data itself through the Hugging Face datasets-server (`/statistics` or `/parquet`) or from the raw CSV at the stated commit, not only from dataset cards. Counts marked "(computed)" are my own aggregation of the raw data and appear in no source.

## Q1. Which GuardBench datasets carry a profanity/obscenity-type label or a specialised-advice/topic label?

### Takeaway
GuardBench itself keeps no categories. It binarises all 40 datasets to safe/unsafe, so any usable category label has to come from the original constituent release. Of the 40, those with relevant labels in the original data are **DoNotAnswer** ("Obscene", "Financial Advice", "Legal Advice", "Medical Advice"), **Do Anything Now Questions** ("Legal Opinion", "Financial Advice", "Health Consultation"), **JBB Behaviors** ("Expert advice"), **CatQA** and **HEx-PHI** ("Tailored Financial Advice"), **DICES 350/990** (per-rater "obscene_and_profane", "obscenities_or_profanities", "medical_legal_financial_or_relationship_advice", "financial_advice"), and **HateCheck** (functionalities `profanity_h` and `profanity_nh`). BeaverTails folds vulgar language into "Hate Speech, Offensive Language". HarmEval probably has a financial-advice category, but I could not verify it because the dataset is gated.

### Inferences
- The best GuardBench-internal source for **denied-topic prompts** is DoNotAnswer's Financial/Legal/Medical Advice subset: 38 prompts with a topic label on the request itself. Next come the Forbidden Question Set's three advice scenarios (90 prompts), CatQA's "Tailored Financial Advice" (50) and HEx-PHI's (30, under a restrictive licence). All of these contain positives only. None provides hard negatives such as general, non-personalised information on the same topic.
- JBB "Expert advice" is the only GuardBench constituent with matched negatives: the benign split holds general-information counterparts of personalised-advice requests. It has only 10 pairs, and several do not fall under financial, medical or legal advice.
- For **profanity presence**, no GuardBench constituent has a clean "text contains profanity" label. HateCheck `profanity_h`/`profanity_nh` is the closest: profanity is present by design in every case of those two functionalities, and hatefulness varies between them. DICES asks about profanity in the chatbot response as a reason for unsafety. Majority agreement on it is nearly absent (computed: 1 of 350 and 0 of 990), so it is a poor positive set.
- I did not check every row of the 25 GuardBench datasets not discussed above. Going by their dataset names and the GuardBench docs, none looks likely to carry a profanity or advice category, but that is inference.

### Gaps
- HarmEval's verbatim categories. The set is gated, and the arXiv abstract page does not list them.
- How DoNotAnswer questions were generated: whether by GPT-4 prompting, human writing, or both. The card does not say, and I did not re-read the paper.
- How the Forbidden Question Set's 30 questions per scenario were produced (LLM-generated or human-written). The card says only "adopted from OpenAI Usage Policy".
- How CatQA prompts were produced. The card does not say.
- The verbatim HateCheck functionality descriptions for `profanity_h`/`profanity_nh` (e.g. "Hate expressed using profanity" / "Non-hateful use of profanity"). These come from memory of the ACL 2021 paper and were not re-fetched.
- AART's policy-concept labels. The Google AART GitHub README fetch returned nothing relevant.

## Q2. Which other guardrail evaluation suites include such categories, and what are their original sources?

### Takeaway
Outside GuardBench, the strongest public sources are these. **NVIDIA Aegis 1.0/2.0 (Nemotron Content Safety V1/V2)** has human-annotated "Profanity" and "Unauthorized Advice" labels. **MLCommons AILuminate v1.0** has human-written prompts labelled `spc_fin`, `spc_hlt`, `spc_lgl` and `spc_ele`. **AIR-Bench 2024** has "Vulgar terms" and "Advice in Heavily Regulated Industries". **SORRY-Bench** covers lewd/obscene words and a high-risk-advice domain. **Salad-Data** has "O17: Law Advice" and "O20: Medical Advice" (auto-labelled). **NVIDIA CantTalkAboutThis** covers topic control. **Civil Comments/Jigsaw** (`obscene`) and **RealToxicityPrompts** (Perspective `profanity`) are the classic presence-style sources. Llama Guard 3, ShieldGemma, WildGuard and Granite Guardian publish taxonomies that include S6 "Specialized Advice" (Llama Guard) and "profanity" (Granite). Llama Guard and Granite release no evaluation data with those labels.

## Q3. For each candidate, is the profanity label about the TEXT containing profanity (presence), about toxicity/offensiveness in general, or about a policy-violation category?

### Takeaway
Almost nothing in these collections is a pure presence label. RealToxicityPrompts `profanity` and Civil Comments `obscene` come closest: both score an attribute of the text, the first with a machine classifier (Perspective API) and the second from crowd raters. HateCheck's profanity functionalities guarantee profanity is present by construction, but the label is about hate. Aegis "Profanity", DICES "obscene_and_profane", DoNotAnswer "Obscene", SORRY-Bench "lewd and obscene words" and AIR-Bench "Vulgar terms" are policy or harm categories. BeaverTails and Granite Guardian fold profanity into offensive language or insults.

### Cited Findings
- **RealToxicityPrompts**: `profanity` is a float attribute on each prompt and continuation, alongside `toxicity`, `insult` and others. This is a per-text attribute score (presence-like, machine-generated) — [datasets-server info](https://datasets-server.huggingface.co/info?dataset=allenai/real-toxicity-prompts)
- **Civil Comments**: `obscene` is a float column next to `toxicity`, `insult` and others. This is a per-comment attribute, crowd-rated (see gaps) — [datasets-server info](https://datasets-server.huggingface.co/info?dataset=google/civil_comments)
- **HateCheck**: `profanity_h` and `profanity_nh` are functionality codes, and `label_gold` is "hateful"/"non-hateful". Profanity is present in every case of both functionalities, and the gold label is hatefulness — [datasets-server statistics](https://datasets-server.huggingface.co/statistics?dataset=Paul/hatecheck&config=default&split=test)
- **Aegis 1.0/2.0 "Profanity"** is a harm category in NVIDIA's "content safety taxonomy". Labels are human-annotated "at the dialog level" and apply to prompt or prompt+response. In V2 the `violated_categories` field lists categories "in order of frequency of annotations" — [Aegis 1.0 card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Aegis-AI-Content-Safety-Dataset-1.0); [Aegis 2.0 card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Aegis-AI-Content-Safety-Dataset-2.0)
- **DICES "obscene_and_profane" / "obscenities_or_profanities"** record "whether the rater perceives the last chatbot response in a conversation to be unsafe because of this reason". This is a per-rater reason-for-unsafety on the response, not on the user text — [350 README](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/dices-dataset/blob/main/350/README.md); [990 README](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/dices-dataset/blob/main/990/README.md)
- **DoNotAnswer "Obscene"**: "Questions containing disgustingly offensive or vulgar language or content." It is a specific-harm class under "Toxic language (hate speech)" and is assigned to the question (prompt) — [HF data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/LibrAI/do-not-answer)
- **SORRY-Bench**: "lewd and obscene words" and "religion-related profane words" are listed as requests to *generate* hate speech. The label marks the kind of request, not the presence of profanity in the prompt — [judge_prompts.jsonl](https://github.com/SORRY-Bench/sorry-bench/blob/7da10addffb6790cfeb75281eaffb5a176861653/data/sorry_bench/judge_prompts.jsonl)
- **AIR-Bench "Vulgar terms"** is an l4 risk category under l3 "Offensive Language" (observed), and marks a request category — [HF data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/stanford-crfm/air-bench-2024)
- **BeaverTails** "Hate Speech, Offensive Language" "also includes language that is considered vulgar, crude, or offensive". It is merged with hate speech and applied to a QA pair — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PKU-Alignment/BeaverTails)
- **Granite Guardian** "Profanity" is defined as "use of offensive language or insults", which conflates insults with profanity — [model card](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-guardian-3.3-8b)
- **Salad-Data** has "O7: Insult" but no profanity class — [datasets-server parquet](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OpenSafetyLab/Salad-Data)

### Inferences
- For a "text contains profanity" reference, the defensible public options are RealToxicityPrompts `profanity` (a machine-model probability, so a proxy, not ground truth) and Civil Comments `obscene` (a rater fraction). Neither comes from a guardrail benchmark collection, and neither is the same concept as profanity presence: obscene ≠ profane.
- Aegis "Profanity" rows can serve as a positive pool for "profane user message", but need re-annotation for strict presence. At least one sampled row is labelled Profanity because it asks for a slur, not because it contains profanity.
- DICES profanity ratings concern chatbot responses. Majority support is near zero (computed), so DICES is unsuitable as a profanity reference.

### Gaps
- The exact annotation question for Civil Comments `obscene` and the Perspective API definition of `PROFANITY`. Not fetched this session.
- Inter-annotator agreement for Aegis "Profanity". Not located.

## Q4. For specialised advice: is the label "the message requests or contains advice on topic X" (denied-topic detection) or "the response is unsafe/non-compliant" (general policy compliance)?

### Takeaway
Most prompt-only sets label the **request's category**: DoNotAnswer, the Forbidden Question Set, CatQA, HEx-PHI, JBB, AIR-Bench, Salad-Data and AILuminate. This is structurally close to denied-topic detection, but every item is a positive (unsafe by construction), and category was assigned by authoring or curation, not by independent per-row annotation. Salad-Data's labels are auto-assigned. DICES and Aegis 2.0 label **harm or violation in a dialog or response**, which is policy compliance, not topic detection. CantTalkAboutThis is the only set built around allowed/disallowed topics in a system policy. JBB's benign split is the only source found with same-topic negatives.

### Cited Findings
- **DoNotAnswer**: the category is on the question. Harm type is "Causing material harm by disseminating misinformation e.g. in medicine or law", and the specific harm definitions say what "AI should not" do, e.g. "AI should not attempt to provide a diagnosis based on symptoms shared by a user" — [HF data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/LibrAI/do-not-answer)
- **Forbidden Question Set**: `content_policy_name` is on the question ("Legal Opinion", "Financial Advice", "Health Consultation"). All 390 are forbidden-scenario questions — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TrustAIRLab/forbidden_question_set)
- **CatQA / HEx-PHI**: "Tailored Financial Advice" is a category of harmful instruction. All samples are unsafe ("Only unsafe samples" per GuardBench) — [CatQA card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/declare-lab/CategoricalHarmfulQA); [HEx-PHI page](https://huggingface.co/datasets/LLM-Tuning-Safety/HEx-PHI); [GuardBench App. A.1](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1022.pdf)
- **JBB "Expert advice"**: each behaviour has a harmful goal (personalised or specific advice) and a benign goal (general information) on the same topic — [HF data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/JailbreakBench/JBB-Behaviors)
- **AILuminate spc_***: `hazard` is the hazard class a human author wrote the prompt for. The prompts were "created to elicit hazardous responses" — [README](https://github.com/mlcommons/ailuminate)
- **Llama Guard 3 S6** is defined over *responses*: "Responses that contain specialized financial, medical, or legal advice …" — [model card](https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-Guard-3-8B)
- **DICES**: `Q2_harmful_content_medical_legal_financial_or_relationship_advice` and `Q2_harmful_content_financial_advice` ask whether "the last chatbot response" is unsafe for this reason. The `harm_type` topic ("Legal", "Health", "Wealth/Finance") is a separate hand-annotated conversation topic — [350 README](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/dices-dataset/blob/main/350/README.md); [990 README](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/dices-dataset/blob/main/990/README.md)
- **Aegis 2.0 "Unauthorized Advice"**: in `violated_categories`, the label is dialog-level, with human prompt labels and response labels from humans or an LLM jury — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Aegis-AI-Content-Safety-Dataset-2.0)
- **AIR-Bench "Advice in Heavily Regulated Industries"** sits under l2 "Operational Misuses", and items are request prompts. Its sibling l3 "Automated Decision-Making" includes "Financing eligibility/Creditworthiness" and similar — [HF data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/stanford-crfm/air-bench-2024)
- **Salad-Data "O17: Law Advice", "O20: Medical Advice"**: category on the question, "auto-labeld" — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OpenSafetyLab/Salad-Data)
- **CantTalkAboutThis**: `system_instruction` states "topics allowed and not allowed", and `distractors` mark off-topic user turns. This is topic-following, where the allowed topic is the in-domain task — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/CantTalkAboutThis-Topic-Control-Dataset)
- **GuardBench**'s binarisation discards every one of these category labels, and the authors acknowledge that finance-specific unsafe content may be missing — [GuardBench paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1022.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Usable as denied-topic positives** (request labelled with topic): DoNotAnswer Financial/Legal/Medical Advice (3/16/19 rows); Forbidden QS Financial Advice / Legal Opinion / Health Consultation (30 each); CatQA Tailored Financial Advice (50); HEx-PHI Tailored Financial Advice (30, restrictive licence); AILuminate demo spc_fin/spc_hlt/spc_lgl (26/26/24, CC-BY-4.0); AIR-Bench Financial/Legal/Medical/Pharmaceutical (15 each plus variants, CC-BY-4.0); Salad-Data Law/Medical Advice (auto-labelled). Most of these sources also contain unrelated or adversarial items (e.g. AILuminate spc_hlt sexual-harm prompts, JBB non-advice items), so row-level review is needed before any is used as a reference.
- **Not denied-topic detection**: DICES advice fields and Aegis 2.0 "Unauthorized Advice" (response or dialog harm), and Llama Guard S6 (response-level, with no public data).
- **Hard negatives** (same topic, general information, not personalised advice) are almost absent. JBB's 10 benign "Expert advice" goals are the only instance found. Building a balanced denied-topic reference would need new negatives.
- **Licence summary** for the advice candidates: MIT (Forbidden QS, JBB), Apache-2.0 (DoNotAnswer, CatQA, Salad-Data, HarmEval), CC-BY-4.0 (AILuminate data, AIR-Bench, DICES, Aegis, CantTalkAboutThis), custom restrictive (HEx-PHI), gated "other" (SORRY-Bench), CC-BY-NC-4.0 (BeaverTails, Toxic Chat), ODC-BY (WildGuardMix).

### Gaps
- Whether the category assignment in DoNotAnswer, Forbidden QS, CatQA and AIR-Bench was checked per row by humans or only set at generation time. The cards do not say.
- Whether AILuminate's full 12,000-prompt Practice set (members only) keeps the same spc_* balance. Not public.
- HarmEval and SORRY-Bench advice-category row counts and verbatim names. Both are gated.
