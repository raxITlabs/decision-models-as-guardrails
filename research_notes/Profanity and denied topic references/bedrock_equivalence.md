# Amazon Bedrock Guardrails: what the PROFANITY word list and denied topics detect, and whether external labels map onto them

Retrieved 2026-09-28 from AWS documentation, AWS blogs, the Bedrock API reference and third-party sources. AWS wording is quoted. Inferences are marked as such.

## Q1. Is Bedrock's profanity filter a word-list match, does AWS publish the list, and what does that imply for human "obscene"/"profane" labels?

### Takeaway
Yes. AWS describes word filters, including the managed PROFANITY list, as exact matching against a list it maintains and does not publish. It supports English, French and Spanish. The docs say nothing about case, plurals, masked spellings (f*ck) or context such as quotation or literal sense. A human "obscene/profane" rating is a judgement about meaning. Bedrock returns a string match against a hidden vocabulary, so the two are not the same task unless the benchmark writes its own definition and scores Bedrock against that definition.

### Cited Findings
- Word filters "block words and phrases (exact match) in input prompts and model responses." — [AWS docs: word filters](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-word-filters.html)
- Profanity filter: "Turn on to block profane words. The list of profanities is based on conventional definitions of profanity and it's continually updated." The console step says the same: "The list of profanity is based on conventional definitions and is continually updated." — [AWS docs: word filters](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-word-filters.html)
- AWS publishes no word list. The docs say only that the list is "based on conventional definitions" and "continually updated". I found no AWS page that enumerates it. — [AWS docs: word filters](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-word-filters.html)
- Because the list is "continually updated", the vocabulary can change over time for the same guardrail version (my inference from that wording). — [AWS docs: word filters](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-word-filters.html)
- Custom word filter: items of "up to three words", with "up to 10,000 items". — [AWS docs: word filters](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-word-filters.html)
- API configuration: `wordPolicyConfig.managedWordListsConfig` takes `type: "PROFANITY"` plus `inputAction`/`outputAction` (`BLOCK | NONE`) and `inputEnabled`/`outputEnabled`. "Use `managedWordListsConfig` to configure a predefined list of profane words." `NONE` means "take no action but return detection information." — [AWS docs: word filters](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-word-filters.html)
- Product page: "Configure filters to help block undesirable words, phrases, and profanity (exact match), including offensive terms, and competitor names." — [AWS Bedrock Guardrails product page](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/guardrails/)
- Languages: the "Word filters language support" table lists English, French and Spanish, each at "Supported" rather than "Optimized and supported". "Supported" is defined as "tested but not tuned for the specific language." The page adds: "Guardrails are ineffective with languages that aren't supported." — [AWS docs: supported languages](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-supported-languages.html)
- Safeguard tiers (Classic/Standard) apply only to content filters, prompt attacks and denied topics. Word filters have no tier. — [AWS docs: safeguard tiers](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-tiers.html)
- How results are reported. The runtime type `GuardrailManagedWord` has `match` ("The match for the managed word", String, required), `type` (valid value `PROFANITY`), `action` (`BLOCKED | NONE`) and `detected` ("Indicates whether managed word content that breaches the guardrail configuration is detected", Boolean, optional). — [API ref: GuardrailManagedWord](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/APIReference/API_runtime_GuardrailManagedWord.html)
- ApplyGuardrail response shape: `assessments[].wordPolicy.managedWordLists: [{ "match": "string", "type": "PROFANITY", "action": "BLOCKED" }]`, next to `customWords: [{ "match", "action" }]`. Top-level `action` is `"GUARDRAIL_INTERVENED" | "NONE"`. — [AWS docs: ApplyGuardrail](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-use-independent-api.html)
- `outputScope: FULL` returns non-detected entries for other policies, but "The full output scope doesn't apply to word filters or regex in sensitive information filters." Word-filter results therefore list only matched strings, with no score and no record of what was checked. — [AWS docs: ApplyGuardrail](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-use-independent-api.html)
- The closest semantic category is the separate content filter **Insults**: "demeaning, humiliating, mocking, insulting, or belittling language. This type of language is also labeled as bullying." None of the content-filter category definitions (Hate, Insults, Sexual, Violence, Misconduct) mention profanity or swear words. — [AWS docs: content filters](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-content-filters.html)
- AWS's 2026 best-practices blog says only that word policies are "commonly used to filter profanity, industry-specific restricted terms, or custom vocabulary restrictions". It gives no detail on matching. — [AWS ML blog, 2 Mar 2026](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/build-safe-generative-ai-applications-like-a-pro-best-practices-with-amazon-bedrock-guardrails/)
- Case sensitivity: a web-search summary attributed to a third-party guide (techjacksolutions.com) said word filters are case-insensitive: "CompetitorX" also catches "competitorx" but not "Competitor X". I could not open the page (HTTP 403), and no AWS source I read states case behaviour. Treat this as unverified. — [techjacksolutions guide (unverified, 403)](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-tools/aws/how-to-use-bedrock-guardrails/)
- Local project evidence, not an AWS source: `benchmark/results/first-benchmark/profanity-obfuscation.json` records `bedrock-apply-words` detecting 3 of 31 masked-spelling rows (detection rate 0.097). The file marks the result "diagnostic, not scored" with "single-AI reference labels (provisional)". Decision models in the same file detected 29–31 of 31. `benchmark/suites/word_filters/PROFANITY.md` already states that the Bedrock answer comes only from the managed PROFANITY list and that its vocabulary is unpublished. — local files in this repo

### Inferences
- (Inference) An "obscene" or "profane" label from human raters, such as the Jigsaw/Civil Comments `obscene` score, is a contextual judgement about whether a text is obscene. Bedrock's PROFANITY list answers a narrower question: does a string from an unpublished list appear in the text? Some cases will diverge for structural reasons: masked spellings, vulgar meaning without a listed word, listed words used in a literal sense ("cock" for a rooster), quotation, and mild words the list may omit. Raw rater labels are not the same task as the Bedrock call. The defensible framing is the one PROFANITY.md already uses: write an explicit word-presence definition, relabel against it, and report Bedrock as "performance against our reference", not as agreement with AWS's vocabulary.
- (Inference) Decision models can be given the benchmark's written definition. Bedrock cannot, because the managed list takes no definition input. The benchmark therefore controls the task statement for one side only. This asymmetry should be disclosed.
- (Inference) "Exact match" together with the local 3/31 masked-spelling result suggests the list does little normalisation of obfuscated spellings. AWS does not document this, and the local labels are provisional.
- (Inference) The response gives a `match` string and no confidence. Bedrock profanity therefore has no threshold to tune, and precision-recall curves cannot be drawn for it. Each call is a single operating point.

### Gaps
- AWS does not publish the PROFANITY word list, its size, its update cadence, or whether updates are versioned.
- No AWS source states case sensitivity, handling of plurals or inflections, tokenisation and punctuation boundaries (whether "cocktail" or "Scunthorpe" can trigger a match), or masked-spelling handling. The case-insensitivity claim is third-party and unverified.
- The docs do not say whether PROFANITY differs by language or whether one list covers EN/FR/ES.
- I found no AWS or third-party accuracy figures for the PROFANITY list specifically.

## Q2. What counts as a denied-topic match, does AWS recommend exceptions in definitions, and how do the Classic and Standard tiers differ?

### Takeaway
A denied topic is a name, a natural-language definition, and up to five optional sample phrases. Bedrock classifies whether a prompt or response "belongs to the topic", judging "contextually". The output is binary (BLOCKED/NONE with `detected`) with no score. AWS explicitly tells users not to write negative definitions or exceptions, not to write instructions, and not to use topics to catch words or entities. Whether a topic catches "requests for advice" or "any mention" is set entirely by how the definition is worded. AWS's own example definition names "inquiries, guidance, or recommendations". Standard tier allows 1,000-character definitions (Classic allows 200), supports far more languages and code, requires cross-Region inference, and AWS claims better recall.

### Cited Findings
**Definition and matching**
- "Model prompts and responses in natural language, as well as code-related content in Standard tier, are evaluated against each denied topic in your guardrail. If one of the denied topics is detected, your guardrail returns a blocked message." — [AWS docs: denied topics](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-denied-topics.html)
- Parameters "which your guardrail uses to detect if a prompt or response belongs to the topic":
  - **Name**: "The name should be a noun or a phrase. Don't describe the topic in the name." (example: "Investment Advice")
  - **Definition**: "Up to 200 characters summarizing the topic content. The definition should describe the content of the topic and its subtopics." Example: "Investment advice is inquiries, guidance, or recommendations about the management or allocation of funds or assets with the goal of generating returns or achieving specific financial objectives."
  - **Sample phrases** (optional): "A list of up to five sample phrases that refer to the topic. Each phrase can be up to 100 characters long. A sample is a prompt or continuation that shows what kind of content should be filtered out." — [AWS docs: denied topics](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-denied-topics.html)
- Limits: "You can add up to 30 denied topics to your guardrail." — [AWS docs: denied topics](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-denied-topics.html)
- API reference `GuardrailTopicConfig`: `name` 1–100 chars, pattern `[0-9a-zA-Z-_ !?.]+`; `definition` "Minimum length of 1. Maximum length of 200"; `examples` 0–5 items, each 1–100 chars, described as "A list of prompts, each of which is an example of a prompt that can be categorized as belonging to the topic"; `type` `DENY`; `inputAction`/`outputAction` `BLOCK | NONE`; `inputEnabled`/`outputEnabled` ("When disabled, you aren't charged for the evaluation"). — [API ref: GuardrailTopicConfig](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/APIReference/API_GuardrailTopicConfig.html)
- Conflict on definition length: the tiers page says Standard allows "Maximum of 1,000 characters per definition" and Classic "Maximum of 200". The denied-topics page and the `GuardrailTopicConfig` API reference both still say 200 with no tier qualifier. — [AWS docs: tiers](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-tiers.html) vs [API ref](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/APIReference/API_GuardrailTopicConfig.html)
- Response: runtime `GuardrailTopic` has `name`, `type` (`DENY`), `action` (`BLOCKED | NONE`) and `detected` ("Indicates whether topic content that breaches the guardrail configuration is detected"). It has no confidence or score field, unlike content filters, which return `confidence` NONE/LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH. — [API ref: GuardrailTopic](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/APIReference/API_runtime_GuardrailTopic.html); [AWS docs: ApplyGuardrail](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-use-independent-api.html)
- `outputScope: FULL` also returns non-detected entries for denied topics. Word filters are the exception. — [AWS docs: ApplyGuardrail](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-use-independent-api.html)
- Ordering affects results: "The order in which the topics are configured can result in varying evaluation outcomes. For example, the same prompt may be blocked under one ordering and allowed under another." — [AWS docs: denied topics](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-denied-topics.html)

**Input vs output**
- Each topic has separate input and output evaluation (`inputEnabled`/`outputEnabled`, `inputAction`/`outputAction`). In ApplyGuardrail, "The source field should be set to `INPUT` when the content to evaluated is from a user … `OUTPUT` when the model output guardrails should be enforced." — [AWS docs: denied topics](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-denied-topics.html); [AWS docs: ApplyGuardrail](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-use-independent-api.html)
- Content filters "do not evaluate tool results (`toolResult`), tool definitions (`toolSpec`), or model-generated tool call arguments". This is stated for content filters. I did not find an equivalent statement for denied topics. — [AWS docs: content filters](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-content-filters.html)

**AWS guidance on writing definitions (quoted)**
- "Define the topic in a crisp and precise manner. A clear and unambiguous topic definition can improve the accuracy of the topic's detection." Example: "Question or information associated with investing, selling, transacting, or procuring cryptocurrencies."
- "Don't include examples or instructions in the topic definition. For example, **Block all contents associated to cryptocurrency** is an instruction and not a definition of the topic."
- "Don't define negative topics or exceptions. For example, **All contents except medical information** or **Contents not containing medical information** are negative definitions of a topic and must not be used."
- "Don't use denied topics to capture entities or words. … The topic definitions represent a theme or a subject and guardrails evaluates an input contextually. Topic filtering should not be used to capture individual words or entity types." It points to sensitive-information filters or word filters for those cases. — [AWS docs: denied topics](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-denied-topics.html)
- The AWS ML blog (2 Mar 2026; Khain, Srinivasan, Rodriguez, Srinivasan) repeats this: "Be crisp and precise", "Define, don't instruct", "Stay positive. Never define topics negatively (for example, 'All content except investment advice')", "Focus on themes, not words", "Provide sample phrases". It also contrasts a precise definition with "vague descriptions, such as 'Investment advice'". — [AWS ML blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/build-safe-generative-ai-applications-like-a-pro-best-practices-with-amazon-bedrock-guardrails/)

**Advice vs mention**
- AWS never states whether a topic should catch requests for advice or any mention. Its worked example builds the scope into the definition ("inquiries, guidance, or recommendations about …"), and the cryptocurrency example uses "Question or information associated with …". The sample phrases are both questions ("Is investing in the stocks better than bonds?", "Should I invest in gold?"). — [AWS docs: denied topics](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-denied-topics.html)

**Tiers**
- Standard: "Provides more robust performance compared to Classic tier and has more comprehensive language and code-related prompt support. … Guardrails with Standard tier also use cross-Region inference. Standard tier supported with content filters and denied topics provides protection against harmful content introduced within code elements including comments, variable and function names, and string literals." Classic: "Provides established guardrails functionality supporting English, French, and Spanish languages." — [AWS docs: tiers](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-tiers.html)
- Tiers table for denied topics: Standard "Maximum of 1,000 characters per definition", Classic "Maximum of 200 characters per definition". Languages: Standard "Extensive", Classic "English, French, Spanish". Cross-Region inference: Standard supported, Classic not. Coding: Standard has "Enhanced support … denied topics", Classic N/A. — [AWS docs: tiers](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-tiers.html)
- `crossRegionConfig` "is required when using the `STANDARD` tier." — [AWS docs: denied topics](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-denied-topics.html)
- Standard-tier denied-topic languages number about 100. Arabic, Chinese (Simplified), Dutch, English, Finnish, French, German, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Norwegian, Polish, Portuguese, Spanish, Swedish and Vietnamese are "Optimized and supported"; the rest are "Supported". Classic covers EN/FR/ES only. — [AWS docs: supported languages](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-supported-languages.html)
- AWS's tier-launch blog (26 Jun 2025) says: "For denied topic detection, the new Standard tier delivers a 32% increase in recall, resulting in an 18% improvement in balanced accuracy." It names no dataset. — [AWS ML blog: safeguard tiers](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/tailor-responsible-ai-with-new-safeguard-tiers-in-amazon-bedrock-guardrails/)

### Inferences
- (Inference) A denied topic is a zero/few-shot semantic classifier driven by the user's definition. Structurally, this is the same kind of task as prompting an LLM decision model with the same definition. An external dataset's label can be posed to both as the same task, provided (a) the benchmark writes the label's meaning as an affirmative definition of 200 characters or fewer (or 1,000 on Standard), (b) the same text goes to the decision models, and (c) exceptions are not written into the Bedrock definition. That last condition breaks symmetry wherever the original label has carve-outs, e.g. "not when used in a literal sense": AWS says not to put exceptions into the definition, while an LLM prompt can hold them.
- (Inference) Because AWS says not to use topics "to capture individual words", posing a word-presence label such as profanity to Bedrock as a denied topic would go against AWS's stated guidance.
- (Inference) Order sensitivity and the Standard/Classic difference both mean the configuration (topic order, tier, definition text, sample phrases, Region profile) belongs in each run's recorded setup.
- (Inference) Topics return no score, so Bedrock gives one operating point per configuration. The NeuralTrust "threshold 0.7" (Q3) cannot be a denied-topic threshold as AWS documents the feature.

### Gaps
- AWS does not say what model powers denied topics, how sample phrases are weighted, or how a text touching several topics is resolved (beyond the ordering note).
- The 200 vs 1,000 character conflict between the API reference and the tiers page is unresolved. Test it with a CreateGuardrail call on each tier.
- No AWS statement on whether the definition's framing ("inquiries … about X" vs "content about X") changes behaviour in practice, i.e. advice vs mention. It is only implied by the examples.
- I found no statement on whether denied topics evaluate tool inputs and outputs. The exclusion is documented only for content filters.
- The re:Post thread "Guardrail negative topics or exceptions" ([link](https://www.repost.aws/questions/QUF89KueARQ7Wh3A6WdnuVFA/guardrail-negative-topics-or-exceptions)) returned HTTP 403, so its answers could not be read.

## Q3. Has AWS or a third party published benchmark results for Bedrock denied topics or profanity, and on which datasets?

### Takeaway
None of the published figures I found use a public, named dataset for denied topics or profanity. AWS offers aggregate marketing numbers ("blocks up to 88% of harmful content"; Standard tier "+32% recall" on denied topics) with no dataset disclosed. Third-party numbers are few and methodologically weak: a vendor blog reports 58% topic accuracy, and a vendor-authored paper tests Bedrock on harmful-content and injection data but does not say which policies were enabled. I found no published profanity-list benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- AWS product page: "blocks up to 88% of harmful content". No dataset or method is given on the page. — [AWS product page](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/guardrails/)
- AWS tier blog: denied topics Standard vs Classic "32% increase in recall, resulting in an 18% improvement in balanced accuracy". No dataset named. — [AWS ML blog, 26 Jun 2025](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/tailor-responsible-ai-with-new-safeguard-tiers-in-amazon-bedrock-guardrails/)
- AWS sample repo `sample-denied-topics-bedrock-guardrails-generator-evaluator`: an LLM (Claude or Nova) generates topic definitions and generates the test prompts, labelled should-pass or should-block. It iterates until accuracy/F1 reach a threshold (default 95%). Sample run: "Accuracy climbed from ~85% to 99%, and F1 from ~80% to 97.6%". The repo cautions that the GenAI model "is inherently non-deterministic. Repeated runs will produce varying results" and advises: "if you already have manually labeled data, you should use that as the ground truth." Test data is synthetic and tuned-on, so the numbers are not independent. — [aws-samples GitHub](https://github.com/aws-samples/sample-denied-topics-bedrock-guardrails-generator-evaluator)
- NeuralTrust (vendor blog, 18 Mar 2025): 2,926 English texts across 14 topical categories (Health & Medicine, Education, Technology, Politics, … Finance & Economy), configured as denied topics. It reports "approximate accuracy of 58%" "with the default threshold value of 0.7", and 0.357 s per sample. Dataset source not stated; no precision, recall or F1. The "0.7 threshold" appears to be the contextual-grounding relevance threshold, not a denied-topic setting. Denied topics expose no threshold in the AWS docs, so the setup is questionable. The task (topic classification of neutral text) also differs from blocking. — [NeuralTrust blog](https://neuraltrust.ai/blog/topic-detection-amazon-bedrock-guardrail)
- "A Comparative Evaluation of AI Agent Security Guardrails" (arXiv 2604.24826): 1,018 entries sampled from ALERT, Salad-Data, Tensor-Trust, PromptWall-Injection, CSSBench, UltraSafety, ToxicQAFinal and Jailbreak-Prompt-Injection (852 BLOCKED, 166 ALLOWED). AWS Bedrock Guardrails recall 743/852 (87.2%), TNR 149/166 (89.8%). The paper describes Bedrock only as "supporting content filtering and contextual groundedness detection" and does not say whether denied topics or word filters were used. The authors are from Beijing Caizhi Tech, associated with the winning product DKnownAI Guard, which is a conflict of interest. — [arXiv 2604.24826](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24826)
- Other comparison pages surfaced in search (Giskard "Best 9 AI guardrails in 2026", aisecbench.com, a Medium comparison, securityopswire.com). I did not read them, so their methodology is unverified. — [Giskard](https://www.giskard.ai/knowledge/ai-guardrails-comparison-2026); [AI Sec Bench](https://aisecbench.com/posts/best-ai-guardrail-tools-review/)
- A DEV Community post reports denied topics blocking a RAG bot's own legitimate questions (anecdotal false positives). I did not read it in full. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/ptokito/bedrock-guardrails-blocked-my-rag-bots-own-questions-denied-topics-and-contextual-grounding-tested-20m9)

### Inferences
- (Inference) No public reference number exists against which a benchmark's Bedrock denied-topic or profanity results could be checked. The benchmark's own frozen runs would be the first dataset-named results for these two policies that I am aware of.
- (Inference) AWS's own evaluation tooling (the sample repo) treats denied-topic accuracy as something to reach by iterating on definitions against labelled data. A benchmark that freezes one definition written in advance measures Bedrock at an untuned operating point. It should state whether definitions were tuned, and on which split.

### Gaps
- I found no AWS-published dataset, per-topic numbers, or false-positive rates for denied topics. The 88% and 32% claims have no stated methodology.
- I found no published benchmark of the managed PROFANITY list from any source.
- I did not verify whether GuardBench or similar public guard benchmarks include Bedrock. No search result showed Bedrock denied topics or profanity on a named public benchmark.
- AWS re:Invent session material was not reviewed within the tool-call budget.
