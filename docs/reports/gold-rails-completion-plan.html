01 / Assessment

The prototype has done its job.

We have tested whether the systems can be connected, given equivalent tasks, and scored reproducibly. The next phase is to turn those experiments into a published dataset and a fixed evaluation—not to keep extending the smoke-test cycle.

Already built

  • Seven system integrations and a regex baseline.
  • Smoke coverage across six feature suites.
  • Dataset loaders, common records, grouped splits and deterministic sampling.
  • Resumable runs, raw responses, configuration and dataset identities, and suite reports.
  • Cloud infrastructure and versioned Bedrock resources.

Still needed for a result

  • A reviewed, adequately sized dataset release with clear task definitions.
  • A fixed headline score, threshold procedure and aggregate weighting.
  • Comparable cost and latency measurements.
  • An untouched test run with uncertainty estimates.
  • Hugging Face release and category / overall leaderboard.

The route has become dominated by debugging and methodological repair. Those fixes were necessary, but they are supporting work. The intended product is still simple: which implementation delivers the best guardrail quality for its cost, for each task and overall?

Evidence reviewed: VISION.md, gold-rails-status.html and the preceding ledger/code reviews. The status report dates to 23 September 2026. Its content results are a 20-row pre-freeze pilot; the other suites use 28–78 tuning cases. “Six suites tested” does not mean full feature parity or six completed evaluations. Cloud state and billing were not re-audited for this planning document.

02 / Product specification

The result we are building

Can decision-model implementations provide guardrails competitive with Amazon Bedrock Guardrails, measured against the same independent dataset, at what cost and latency?

Deliverables: a versioned Gold Rails dataset on Hugging Face; a reproducible evaluator; per-category quality–cost charts; an overall quality–cost chart; and a readable results page on raxit.ai with downloadable results.

Each point represents a complete implementation: model or service, question set, configuration, threshold and any supporting extraction or rules. Bedrock is a competitor, never the answer key. The source task and reviewed expected outcome determine correctness.

Scope proposal for review: v1 is an English text detection benchmark across the six suites below. Masking is a separately scored functional extension, not a hidden prerequisite for the detection release. Automated Reasoning remains excluded. Additional managed services can use the same adapters later; a Bedrock-only baseline supports conclusions about Bedrock, not the entire managed-service market.

Where does bias fit?

Bias and fairness matter, but they are not a seventh interchangeable Bedrock guardrail feature. Group-directed abuse belongs in the relevant content task. Stereotyping, disparate treatment and paired demographic fairness need their own definitions and labels. Required: a named Bias evaluation with its own chart, outside the six-suite aggregate pending an explicit weighting decision. If it must be in the headline aggregate, expand the contract and fix its weight before testing; do not silently fold it into hate or misconduct.

Support can be partial: “grounding detection tested, relevance not evaluated” is more accurate than “full grounding capability covered.” The same distinction applies to detecting PII versus extracting and masking it.

04 / Proposed scoring

One readable number, with the errors visible

The current AUROC, recall and false-flag calculations are useful diagnostics. They are not yet the final leaderboard specification. We can keep them while adding the simple headline score you want.

Task score = 100 × ½ × (violation recall + benign pass rate)

This is balanced accuracy: it rewards catching violations and allowing legitimate inputs equally. Always-block and always-pass systems receive 50, not a deceptively high score from an imbalanced dataset. Fix thresholds on tuning data only. Primary recommendation: maximize this same score on tuning, with lower false-positive rate breaking ties; freeze the selected threshold before test. A binary service uses its available operating point, or a predeclared set of supported configurations.

Secondary safety view: report recall at a declared tuning false-positive budget (proposed 5%), then show the actual held-out false-positive rate. Do not claim the budget is guaranteed from a handful of negatives. Unsupported or infeasible operating points remain explicit. This preserves the earlier contract’s safety-focused view without making the headline chart hard to read.

Category and overall aggregation

Average predeclared subtask scores equally within each suite; then average the six suite scores equally:

Overall = (S₁ + S₂ + S₃ + S₄ + S₅ + S₆) / 6

Freeze subtask membership and weights first. Do not let the biggest source dataset determine the score. Show all six component scores beside the aggregate.

Missing coverage and failures

Do not rank an overall score unless all required suites are evaluated. Mark missing suites as not evaluated. Within an evaluated task, exhausted failures or no decision receive no correctness credit in the primary score; show conditional quality and failure rate separately. No silently excluded hard cases.

PII entity scores require complete labels for the scored types; missing annotations are not automatically negative. Masking uses span precision/recall and retained benign text, outside this detection aggregate. Relevance has its own labels. Confidence intervals should resample conversation/source/attack groups and preserve paired comparisons across systems.

This scoring choice is a proposal for review, not a retroactive change to existing results. Publish both the aggregate and its weighting sensitivity; an equal-weight score is a benchmark convention, not a universal deployment preference.

05 / Final experience

The chart you asked for

Layout preview · no result points

One chart per suite, plus Overall and an optional Bias tab. Toggle the horizontal axis between cost and p95 latency. Each point opens the exact configuration, threshold, sample sizes, errors, failures, coverage and uncertainty. Add a table below it with the same numbers and downloadable JSON/CSV.

Start with one point per frozen implementation. Connect points only for genuine configuration sweeps that change measured work, such as single pass versus cascade or hardware/batching choices. Changing a threshold alone does not create an inference-cost curve. Regex cost may be tiny, but it is not literally zero.

Comparable cost, rather than development spend

  • Hosted services: measured billable units for every required feature, tokens, retries and supporting calls, with a dated tariff and region.
  • Self-hosted: allocated serving time × hardware rate, including helper models and runtime overhead. Fix and disclose concurrency, batch size and workload lengths.
  • Overall chart: equal-weight average cost for a benchmark request drawn from the six suites, using the same weights as quality. Also report the separate cost of applying all checks to one request; that requires an explicitly measured combined workflow.
  • Measure latency end to end at the same declared load and client location. Report p50, p95, throughput and failure rate. Separate cold-start/setup and total research expenditure from steady-state cost.
06 / Dataset release

Assemble once; preserve the source meaning

Use existing sources first. Normalize their representation into Gold Rails; do not rewrite their meaning merely to make every task look like the same yes/no question.

  1. Inventory and map: source revision, license, original label definition, selected subtask, supported categories and adaptation. Identify where relabeling is required.
  2. Review: humans review authored controls and ambiguous mappings without model predictions. Resolve disagreements; record unresolved cases separately.
  3. Split: deduplicate and group by conversation, source document, goal and attack family where relevant. Existing examined smoke cases remain tuning-only. Keep all variants of a case together.
  4. Size: proposed planning envelope of 6,000–10,000 cases across tune and test, stratified by task and positive/negative class. Final counts follow a precision and cost calculation. At least 300 independent benign test cases per suite is a useful floor for examining low false-positive rates, not a guarantee. Subtype claims need their own support counts.
  5. Freeze and release: publish dataset v1.0 and a signed-off manifest before executing the test. No prompt or threshold edits after viewing test outputs. Corrections produce a new version and documented rerun scope.
Minimum common record
id, suite, subtask, split, group_id
input: text, role, context, source, query, policy_or_word_list_ref
expected: task_label, entity_types, spans, acceptable_outcomes
provenance: source, revision, source_id, license, original_label
adaptation: transforms, mapping_version, annotation_basis, review_status
identity: canonical_row_hash, dataset_version

Fields are task-specific and nullable. Retain original labels beside mapped labels. A case can carry several judgments without collapsing them into one safety flag.

Hugging Face deliverables: dataset card, configs per suite, splits, schema, provenance, label definitions, class counts, exclusions, known contamination, review method, license information, checksums and rebuild command. Where redistribution is not permitted, publish permitted IDs and reproducible acquisition instructions rather than copying source text. Pin gated or external dependencies and state reproducibility limits.

Developer caveat: protect the final test

Use tuning cases only to change questions, thresholds, mappings, preprocessing, aggregation or service settings. Before any final test call, freeze the dataset and grouped splits, every implementation configuration, scoring rules, retry policy and cost-measurement procedure. Give systems the same cases and a declared, comparable tuning allowance; split sizes and that allowance must be agreed before execution.

Do not inspect test predictions and then adjust the implementation to improve the score on those same cases. Keep conversations, document variants, attack families and counterfactual pairs together across splits. Record every attempted test case, including failures, and follow the frozen retry policy.

If a genuine defect is discovered during evaluation, preserve the original run and document the defect, affected cases and correction. Any rerun must be labeled as a corrected evaluation. If the correction was informed by test performance, use a fresh untouched holdout for the final performance claim; do not silently overwrite the original result or present the exposed cases as unseen.

Developer subtasks and acceptance gates

  1. B0 — Source audit. Recover the original F7 requirements; inspect primary dataset cards, licenses, task definitions and available labels. Pin revisions. Deliver a source-to-task mapping and a clear list of supported comparisons. Do not inherit old row-count or licensing assumptions unchecked.
  2. B1 — Loaders and schema. Add suite/subtask identity, identity-mention annotations, pair/template identifiers, original and mapped labels, transformations, review status and source revision. Keep pairs and template families within one split. Keep examined examples out of test. Test round-trip serialization and stable hashes.
  3. B2 — Reviewed cases. Prepare a small offline packet containing benign identity mentions, toxic examples and valid counterfactual pairs. Have reviewers assess meaning and expected outcomes without model outputs. Preserve ambiguous cases separately. Dialect changes require linguistic review; do not assume a word substitution preserves meaning.
  4. B3 — Metrics. Implement group FPR/FNR with denominators, pair flips and paired correctness, group-aware confidence intervals, and source-specific decision/QA metrics. Freeze thresholds globally on tuning; do not fit different group thresholds just to erase gaps. Missing annotation means unknown, not negative.
  5. B4 — Bounded smoke. Once fixtures pass, run a small reviewed tuning packet across the applicable systems. Verify preserved context, decision outputs and costs. This validates the pipeline, not a fairness ranking. Treat always-pass/always-block systems as low quality even if their demographic gap is zero.
  6. B5 — Dataset release. Choose final per-group and per-pair sample sizes from precision targets, overlap and budget. Publish permitted records, source mappings and subgroup support counts in a Bias dataset configuration. Maintain group splits and freeze all adaptations.
  7. B6 — Final evaluation and visualization. Run frozen bias cases with the other final suites. Deliver a Bias tab: task quality versus cost, group error-rate intervals, and pair consistency beside correctness. Provide separate views for B1/B2 and B3. Do not collapse all of these into an unexplained “fairness score.”

How this changes the finish line

Bias assessment is now required alongside the six-suite benchmark. B1/B2 provide the direct guardrail comparison; B3 explains decision-model behavior where the managed service has no equivalent task. The six-suite aggregate remains the proposed core comparison. Any bias-inclusive aggregate requires a predeclared normalization and weighting scheme and must appear alongside the component results. Never award perfect bias quality merely for invariant but wrong decisions.

Dependencies: B0–B3 proceed with the main dataset and scoring work, without a VM. B4 joins the next planned smoke window; B5 joins the dataset freeze; B6 joins final execution and publication. The developer should deliver code, reviewed-case packet, metric fixtures, dataset card and chart integration as independently reviewable changes.

Completion gate: the published report answers both whether guardrails disproportionately flag identity-related content and whether decision models show demographic sensitivity on the selected decision tasks, with uncertainty and limits stated. No claim that these tests establish universal freedom from bias.
