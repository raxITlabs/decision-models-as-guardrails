Task 1: the guardrail golden dataset

Plan for approval · 22 September 2026, revised the same evening for source freshness and policy adherence · precedes any harness or adapter work

Organise the dataset around the features people pay Amazon Bedrock Guardrails for, add bias as a first-class feature because a decision model's number can hide it, and build it the way JevBench built its task set: one record schema, human labels only in the headline, a held-out slice, hashes frozen before any system runs. The capability matrix, what each system can and cannot do, falls out of the dataset rather than being asserted.

1. Features, taken from what Bedrock sells

Seven Bedrock pillars, minus the two we cannot serve (Automated Reasoning, images), plus two the benchmark adds: bias and actions. Each feature gets its own slice, its own label semantics, and its own metrics.

2. Bias, designed as a feature rather than a caveat

A guardrail is biased when it treats the same content differently depending on who is mentioned, or when a decision it makes depends on a protected attribute that should not matter. A decision model returns a number and nothing else, so the only way to find this is to measure it. Three sub-tests, each with a different mechanism.

B1 · disparate false positives

Benign text that mentions an identity group. Source: google/civil_comments identity-annotated rows (CC0, 1.8M, we sample ~1,500 benign across ~20 identity attributes). Metric: false-positive rate per identity group on F1 categories, and the largest gap. This is the Jigsaw unintended-bias design, applied to guardrails.

B2 · counterfactual pairs

Take ~1,000 F1 rows and ~300 F8 rows, swap a name, dialect marker, or group descriptor using the HolisticBias descriptor list (~600 descriptors, CC-BY-SA) and CrowS-Pairs style templates. Same content, different group. Metric: absolute score delta per attribute, and flip rate at the tuned threshold. Half the templates are held out.

B3 · decision bias

Anthropic/discrim-eval (CC-BY-4.0): 70 decision scenarios (loan, housing, hiring, visa) with age, gender, race varied. Asked as a Choice or Noul ("should this be approved"), which is exactly how a decision model would be used. Metric: discrimination score as in the paper. heegyu/bbq adds ambiguous-context stereotype reliance for Choice questions.

What bias is not, here

We do not measure whether a model's opinions are biased. We measure whether a guardrail's decisions change with protected attributes when the content does not. That is narrow, defensible, and what a buyer would be liable for.

3. Sources: freshness first

The field's data is stale, and that is the opportunity. A catalogue of 195 safety benchmarks released 2018 to 2026 found 137 with inactive repositories and 96 with outdated Hugging Face datasets. Qwen3Guard's own report lists ToxicChat, the 2022 OpenAI moderation set, Aegis, SimpleSafetyTests, HarmBench, WildGuardTest, XSTest and BeaverTails as its evaluation sets, and DynaGuard trained on WildGuard, BeaverTails, ToxicChat and Aegis 2.0. A November 2025 study of ten guard models found Qwen3Guard fell from 91.0% on benchmarks to 33.8% on novel prompts, a 57-point gap the authors attribute to training-data contamination. Even the August 2026 EMNLP paper "No One Model Catches Every Harm" collected no new data and reused the same eleven sets. So: the classic sets stay only as an anchor slice, so our numbers can be checked against published ones; the headline is built from 2025 to 2026 sources, our own crafted rows, and a private slice, on the Lakera PINT principle that "all evaluated solutions are not directly trained on any of the inputs".

Headline versus anchor

Headline rows are 2025 or later, human-labelled or deterministic, or ours. Anchor rows are the classic sets, kept at about 2k rows so every system's number can be compared to its published one, and reported in a separate table. A system that does well on anchor and badly on headline has memorised the benchmarks; that gap is itself a published column.

Private slice, PINT style

About 20% of headline rows are never released: half the counterfactual templates, half the crafted policies, all crafted F8 scenarios, and a random 10% of every other headline feature. They go only to production APIs and our own machines, following JevBench's rule, and are refreshed yearly. Lakera has kept PINT private since 2024 for exactly this reason.

Crafting is now a bigger share

Roughly 6k of 32k test rows are ours. Every crafted row carries a rationale and a reviewer, JevBench's hard-tier discipline, and the generator prompts are published so the method is reproducible even where the rows are private.

What is not fresh, and why we keep it anyway

civil_comments (2019) for identity-conditioned false positives and discrim-eval (2023) for decision bias have no 2026 replacement of comparable size and labelling. They are used with fresh crafted rows beside them and the age stated.

4. The record

Borrowed from JevBench's tasks.py and extended for guardrail semantics. One dataclass, one validator, one hash.

record = {
  id:          "f1-aegis-0413",
  feature:     "F1" | "F2" | ... | "F8",
  subtask:     "input" | "output" | "over_refusal" | "jailbreak" | "injection" | "leakage" | "indirect"
             | "topic" | "word" | "pii" | "grounding" | "relevance" | "b1" | "b2" | "b3" | "action",
  state:       { role, text, context: [ {role, text} ], tool_call?, source?, query? },
  category:    { ailuminate: "violent_crimes", bedrock: "VIOLENCE", source_label: "Criminal Planning" },
  labels:      ["no", "yes"]  or  ordered options / levels,
  expected:    "yes" | option | level index | spans[] | null,
  group:       "b2-name-swap-0091" | null,          # counterfactual or paraphrase pair
  attribute:   { kind: "gender", value: "female" } | null,   # F7 only
  split:       "tune" | "test",
  visibility:  "public" | "heldout",
  provenance:  { source, source_id, licence, label_basis: "human"|"deterministic"|"synthetic_reviewed",
                 imported_at, contamination: ["llama-guard-3", ...], exclude_reason, notes }
}
  • validate() refuses an expected outside labels, a state carrying expected, label, answer, or ground_truth, and an F7 row with no attribute.
  • dataset_hash() is order-independent; manifest.json pins one hash per feature per split, written before any system runs.
  • label_basis other than human or deterministic excludes the row from the headline and keeps it in an appendix. That is the answer to "your answer key came from an LLM" before it is asked.
  • Questions are not in the record. The record is what is judged; the question set each system receives is a separate versioned file, so the same rows can be re-asked when a question set changes.

7. Four weeks

  1. Week 1. Schema, validator, hash, manifest. Loaders for the F1 and F2 public sources. Category mapping file, first version. Licence table committed.
  2. Week 2. Loaders for F5, F6, F7 sources. Dedup and contamination flags. Crafted F3 topic definitions and F4 obfuscation generator, reviewed.
  3. Week 3. F7 counterfactual generator over HolisticBias descriptors, human review of 300 pairs. F8 scenarios. Audit round: two annotators, 5% per source, κ reported.
  4. Week 4. Split, freeze, datasheet, public release of the public slice on Hugging Face. Run the tune split through Jev and OpenAI moderation only, to fill the capability matrix and catch schema mistakes before any ranking exists.

8. Decisions needed

  1. Confirm the eight features and the row targets, and that Automated Reasoning and images are out of scope.
  2. Bias sub-tests: all three in v1, or B1 and B3 first with B2's counterfactual generation in a second pass.
  3. Non-commercial sources (ToxicChat, BeaverTails): include in the headline with NC flagged, or appendix only. Include changes what a commercial user of the set can do with it.
  4. Annotation: in-house for the 5% audit and the crafted rows, or a paid vendor. About 1,800 rows to re-label plus 300 counterfactual pairs to review.
  5. Held-out policy: keep a private slice indefinitely (JevBench's choice), or publish everything after the first results.
  6. Name for the dataset. Working title "Gold Rail".
