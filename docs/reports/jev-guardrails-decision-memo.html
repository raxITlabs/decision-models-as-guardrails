Should raxIT Labs build an open-source guardrail on Jev?

Decision memo · 21 September 2026 · six days after Jev's release · sources: TypeSafe docs, AWS docs, 8 community repos, 3 practitioner benchmarks, our own runs, and seven 2026 industry surveys (~5,000 respondents)

Yes, as a research project with a hard go/no-go, and not for the buyer we first had in mind. Jev is a fast, cheap, calibrated classifier. It is not a guardrail on its own. The product is the system around it, and the honest pitch is "custom policy without retraining, with tool-call visibility Bedrock lacks", proven by a comparative eval nobody has published yet.

2. How it works: the mechanism, not the name

Jev takes a state and a map of typed questions, and returns one typed number per question. It never blocks, never explains, never generates. Everything that looks like a decision is code we write around those numbers.

The system that makes it a guardrail

Four tiers. Each exists because a measured failure needs it: code for what code can decide, Jev for indicator extraction, a decider that owns the verdict, and an escalation path for what the question set did not anticipate.

4. Who is this for? The hypothesis against fresh 2026 research

Sources: seven surveys published between December 2025 and August 2026, about 5,000 respondents in total. No internal research was used in this section.

Half right. The hypothesis was a CTO of a 500 to 1,500 person company whose top concerns are responsibility checks, transparency, and evals. The research supports the title and the size band, and it supports "responsibility" if that means a named owner and a kill switch. It does not support "evals" as a felt pain, and "transparency" shows up as audit trails and visibility into what agents did, not as explainability. The buyer's own words are about control at runtime, over-privileged agents, and quality.

ICP, as the research supports it

Title

CTO, VP Engineering, or Head of Platform at a company that builds and runs agents. They own the model traffic and today they rely on the provider's native controls.

Size

250 to 2,000 employees. Below 2,000 security is not yet the primary blocker and there is rarely a CISO org to defer to; above it, procurement and the security team take over.

Pain to lead with

"My agents are in production, half of them are unmonitored, and I have no gate between a policy decision and an action." Runtime control and a decision log, in their words, not "evals".

Trigger

A near-miss. 54 to 87% report one in the last year. The second trigger is a policy the provider's fixed categories can't express.

Where the original hypothesis was wrong: evals are our language, not the buyer's. They buy the outcome (fewer bad actions, proof of what happened) and we deliver it with evals underneath. "Transparency" should be written as audit trail and visibility. And the number one organisational problem, that no one owns agent behaviour, is something a tool can support but not solve.

5. The use case, and why the open-source guardrail is still the right one

We considered a consumer paste guard (the market went local-first and OpenAI shipped a free open-weight PII model in April), a fun native app (good showcase, not a business), and slop detection (unsolved, same architecture, no privacy contradiction). The guardrail wins because it is the only one that lands on a recorded buyer pain, reuses everything built so far, and produces a research artefact nobody else has: a measured Jev safety benchmark.

Open source, what it is for

Trust and distribution into engineering teams that today rely on provider-native controls (82% in the June 2026 pulse). The metric is teams running it against their own agent traffic, not stars. A guardrail API with a published eval is exactly that shape.

What it is not

Not a Bedrock clone. Not a claim to be the best hate-speech classifier; a fine-tuned encoder may beat Jev there and the eval will say so. The claim is custom policy, tool-call visibility, and one endpoint.

Showcase

Slop detection for X and LinkedIn as the fun demo of the identical pipeline: code features plus indicator Nouls plus a small classifier. Public data, no privacy argument, no measured version exists.

Research question

"If you use Jev as your guardrail, how good is it against Bedrock, Llama Guard 3, OpenAI Moderation, and a trained local encoder, per category, at what cost and latency, and where does it break?"

7. What would kill it

  • Adversarial collapse. If the in-the-wild jailbreak set drops Jev's recall far below Llama Guard's, the prompt-attack pillar is gone and the pitch shrinks to content plus custom topics plus tool calls. Still a product, smaller.
  • The local baseline wins everywhere. If a 22M encoder beats Jev on every fixed category, Jev's role narrows to custom categories and zero-shot policy. The eval must be designed to show that clearly rather than bury it.
  • Bedrock closes the gap. AgentCore Policy already puts guardrail scores inside Cedar. If AWS adds custom categories and tool-argument scanning, the differentiation is calibration and price only.
  • No one owns the budget. Only 7.2% of organisations name an accountable owner for agent behaviour, and a third spend 5% or less of security budget on agents. A free tool the CTO's team adopts sidesteps procurement; that is the open-source argument, and it needs to be proven with real adoption, not assumed.
  • Rate limits and model drift. TypeSafe says limits change without notice and aliases move. Pin jev-1.13.0, record the model per result, re-run the harness on each release.

8. Decisions needed

  1. Approve the ICP as the research supports it: CTO, VP Eng, or Head of Platform at agent builders of 250 to 2,000 people, runtime control and a decision log as the lead pain, evals as our mechanism rather than the pitch.
  2. Approve the eval as the first deliverable and the go/no-go criteria above as pre-registered.
  3. Approve AWS credentials and a modest Bedrock budget so the comparison is measured, not quoted.
  4. Decide whether slop detection ships as the public showcase in weeks 6 to 8 or waits for a go.
